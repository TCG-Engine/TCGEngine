## Added families

### F41: Damage without an attack

**Gap:** Pure healing or block is not a substitute for Guarded Strike’s direct retaliation. It uses combat damage, not total damage, to qualify.

**Family design:** Give retaliation its own family and keep the no-combat-damage check. Both sides check the same combat snapshot; direct retaliation cannot recurse.

**Still to test:** Test a mirrored retaliation, a penetrating hit, and a blocked attack. Apply both retaliations before final exchange objectives and knockout checks.

Current examples: Guarded Strike.

### F42: Deny the next burn

**Gap:** Clinch Throw’s next-round burn lock was absent. Temporary exhaustion and defensive status penalties do not reproduce that denial.

**Family design:** Separate bonus-only, reaction-only, and both-mode locks, all lasting exactly one next round. Main play and advantage spending remain available.

**Still to test:** Spend on reveal even if the hit fails. Test both priorities, next-hand movement, overlapping locks, and whether the victim retains useful non-burn plans.

Current examples: Clinch Throw.

### F43: Read the defensive investment

**Gap:** A general bonus-commitment read missed Grip Break’s narrower requirement: the resolved bonus must give its own player block.

**Family design:** Keep defensive commitment separate from reaction block, bonus gifts, and general bonus presence. Vary the reward, not the meaning of the condition.

**Still to test:** Check conditional bonus branches, hybrid bonuses, attack-only bonuses, and bonuses gifting block to the other fighter.

Current examples: Grip Break.

### F44: Meet the incoming pressure

**Gap:** Reading printed attack does not cover Grip Break’s read of pressure carried into the exchange.

**Family design:** Read only attack that entered this round. Current bonuses and rewards being earned for next round do not qualify.

**Still to test:** Compare carried amounts 1, 2, and 4 with an equal-sized current-round boost. Test Brace-like suppression before valuing the read.

Current examples: Grip Break.

### F45: Change the revealed plan

**Gap:** Telegraphing and cooldowns do not cover Darting Strike’s post-reveal cancel. It changes the main after gaining information and permanently loses the original.

**Family design:** All replacements keep the committed bonus and carry, cannot spend advantage, and recalculate opposing printed reads. Both choices stay hidden until finished.

**Still to test:** A spent token is not refunded on Keep or a failed condition. Burn locks prevent the cancel. Test simultaneous replacements and an already-burned bonus being ineligible.

Current examples: Darting Strike.

### F46: Choose a small wound

**Gap:** The refinement removed an exact-one stay condition but also left Glancing Step’s important exact-damage incentive without a family.

**Family design:** Keep exact damage distinct from at-most damage. Use final exchange damage after retaliation, not merely combat damage.

**Still to test:** Test incoming damage 0/1/2/3 and reactions that move the exchange off the target. Damage-over-time ticks do not satisfy these draft conditions.

Current examples: Glancing Step.

### F47: Choose their destination

**Gap:** Cutting one route is not the same as Driving Kick choosing the actual opposing destination.

**Family design:** Force only after route additions and cuts; preserve the existing possibility of forcing an empty printed destination. Do not invent an unrelated destination.

**Still to test:** Test one remaining route, an empty legal destination, both fighters forcing, and protection against forced choices. No hidden destination may be inspected early.

Current examples: Driving Kick.

### F48: Set tomorrow’s objective

**Gap:** Delayed healing and Heavy telegraphs do not represent Rooted Guard’s independent next-round objective.

**Family design:** Setup requires neither hitting nor taking no damage. It follows hand changes and expires after one following round, whether successful or denied.

**Still to test:** Current-round damage cannot earn it. Test renewal, an already-held Green token, Counterpunch denial, and separate setup and reward rounds.

Current examples: Rooted Guard.

### F49: Reset the pressure

**Gap:** Cleansing conditions was not a replacement for Brace. Brace changes carry timing and affects both players’ future gains.

**Family design:** Separate incoming suppression from future prevention. Own incoming carry remains; delayed objectives are not cancelled.

**Still to test:** Test own versus opposing carry, carry earned by taking exactly 1 damage, a Darting replacement, and future bonuses already prevented this round.

Current examples: Brace.

### F50: Take away the held reward

**Gap:** Denying a new award does not reproduce Mirror Step’s removal of already-held tokens.

**Family design:** Choose held tokens after damage and before new objective awards. A token spent on reveal is already gone; independent triggers can remove two.

**Still to test:** Test no held tokens, one versus two targets, simultaneous stripping, new awards, and a knockout before any meaningful future spend.

Current examples: Mirror Step.

### F51: End the reusable threat

**Gap:** Self-exhaustion and relocation do not cover Charged Fist destroying a reusable opposing main.

**Family design:** The destroyed main completes its simultaneous damage, main effects, and objective first. Destruction occurs instead of its normal return.

**Still to test:** Test simultaneous destruction, a failed hit with Purple still spent, the last surviving card, and a target that was replaced before combat.

Current examples: Charged Fist.

### F52: Read the advantage spend

**Gap:** Token holding, generating, cleansing, and spending are different events. Heavy Feint reads the actual committed spend.

**Family design:** Count consumed reveal-time spends even if their conditional payoff fails. Spending two still triggers an any-spend read only once.

**Still to test:** Test held-but-unspent, newly earned, stripped, one spent, two spent, and Yellow consumed before a Darting replacement.

Current examples: Heavy Feint.

### F53: Punish the small attack

**Gap:** High-threshold reads do not cover Off-Balance Sweep and Forearm Check punishing small attacks.

**Family design:** Hold base stats fixed and change the ceiling and reward in opposite directions. A boosted low printed attack remains a qualifying read.

**Still to test:** Compare printed attacks 1/3/5/6, then add carry or reaction attack without changing the printed value.

Current examples: Off-Balance Sweep, Forearm Check.

### F54: Read an open guard

**Gap:** Catch the Opening’s low-block attack read and Driving Kick’s low-block defensive read were missing.

**Family design:** Show both possible rewards for reading weak guard. They need not share the same purpose merely because their condition overlaps.

**Still to test:** Test printed block 1/2/3/4 with both a heavy opposing attack and a quiet one. Later block buffs do not change the read.

Current examples: Driving Kick, Catch the Opening.

### F55: Name the printed guard

**Gap:** The previous exact-read family tested only attack. Mirror Step independently reads printed block exactly 3.

**Family design:** Use three exact block targets with equal rewards. Keep printed values distinct from temporary block and penetration.

**Still to test:** Measure actual target frequency in the active hand. Test the correct value, adjacent values, and an unchanged printed value with reaction block.

Current examples: Mirror Step.

### F56: Commit a conditional bonus

**Gap:** Fixed burns omitted Feint, Find the Gap, and Read the Strike’s conditional bonus branches.

**Family design:** Keep the main fixed and vary the permanently burned bonus. Its conditional reward is chosen before the opposing main and bonus are revealed.

**Still to test:** Test the resolved branch, the weak fallback, and the permanent loss. Do not apply a card’s own bonus to itself as main.

Current examples: Feint, Read the Strike, Find the Gap.

### F57: React to what was revealed

**Gap:** Bait and Slip and Read the Strike have conditional reactions, not just conditional mains or bonuses.

**Family design:** Keep a useful fallback and evaluate the reaction after reveal. Its known condition gives it different risk from the face-down conditional bonus.

**Still to test:** Compare actual reaction turns and competing burns. Conditional reaction efficiency still spends a permanent card and cannot create hidden information.

Current examples: Bait and Slip, Read the Strike.

### F58: A missing mode is a cost

**Gap:** Giving every draft both modes erased a real restriction on current cards, including mains with no bonus or no reaction.

**Family design:** Print unavailable modes explicitly. The improved main sacrifices a different possible permanent use; it does not get a free bonus effect.

**Still to test:** Test a hand that needs reaction defense, one that needs bonus fuel, and late-game card conservation. Unavailable is not a zero-cost activation.

Current examples: Quick Jab, Rushdown, Guarded Strike, Clinch Throw, Driving Tackle, Grip Break, Counter Throw, Darting Strike, Driving Kick, Forearm Check, Brace, Haymaker, Crushing Blow.

### F59: Two independent reads

**Gap:** One condition granting two stats is different from Mirror Step and Grip Break having independently evaluated conditions.

**Family design:** Make independence explicit. One branch failing never prevents the other, and both may apply. An OR objective still awards at most its stated token reward.

**Still to test:** Test neither, each condition alone, and both. Confirm additive stat rewards without accidentally duplicating an objective award.

Current examples: Grip Break, Mirror Step.

### F60: Pay only when you miss

**Gap:** The refined commitment family intentionally made the penalty unconditional, leaving Haymaker’s actual miss penalty unrepresented.

**Family design:** Keep a separate miss-cost family rather than treating the two timings as interchangeable. Direct retaliation does not turn a zero-combat-damage miss into a hit.

**Still to test:** Measure how often each attack really misses after reactions. Inspect next-round zero-block profiles where the printed penalty may have little value.

Current examples: Haymaker.

### F61: Earn from the matchup

**Gap:** A generic deal-damage ladder did not cover the current set’s matchup-dependent and clean-outcome objectives.

**Family design:** Keep objective evaluation after final exchange damage, with held-color caps and simultaneous denial. Exact original objective wording remains on every current-card baseline.

**Still to test:** Test printed conditions versus final damage, direct retaliation, already-held colors, Counterpunch denial, and simultaneous success.

Current examples: Guarded Strike, Feint, Covering Step, Body Lock, Grip Break, Off-Balance Sweep, Counter Throw, Sidestep, Bait and Slip, Brace, Read the Strike, Watchful Guard, Crushing Blow.

### F62: Earn by conserving

**Gap:** No-bonus stat boosts were not the same mechanic as Haymaker and Measured Advance earning resources for different kinds of conservation.

**Family design:** Separate no bonus, no reaction, and no own burns. Opposing burns never disqualify your conservation objective.

**Still to test:** Test each own mode separately and opposing burns. A bonus sacrifice cannot be undone just to restore the objective condition.

Current examples: Measured Advance, Haymaker.

### F63: Read any bonus commitment

**Gap:** The refined bonus-read family only varied block. Watchful Guard and Firm Counter also reward attack or both stats.

**Family design:** Represent all three payoff shapes without changing the underlying committed-bonus predicate. Reactions do not satisfy it.

**Still to test:** Test bonus and no-bonus commitments, then how each allocation changes both fighters’ damage thresholds.

Current examples: Firm Counter, Watchful Guard.

### F64: Allocate the bonus

**Gap:** Attack-fuel and reaction families did not directly represent the current cards’ defensive and mixed precommitted bonuses.

**Family design:** Compare three bonus allocations with identical mains, reactions, and routes. Mixed fuel uses two points rather than three because two relevant thresholds can make flexibility valuable.

**Still to test:** Test lethal trades, surplus attack/block, defensive-bonus reads, and the opportunity cost of committing before seeing the opposing plan.

Current examples: Quick Jab, Combination Strike, Covering Step, Body Lock, Off-Balance Sweep, Counter Throw, Sidestep, Darting Strike, Bait and Slip, Outside Step, Glancing Step, Driving Kick, Rooted Guard, Forearm Check, Firm Counter, Driving Palm, Measured Advance, Catch the Opening, Watchful Guard, Mirror Step, Counterpunch, Charged Fist, Iron Guard, Heavy Feint.

### F65: Hit into the heavy attack

**Gap:** The original high-attack family granted only block. Trade Blows, Rooted Guard, Counter Throw, and Read the Strike also turn that read into attack.

**Family design:** Keep attack rewards distinct from defensive counters, while linking cards that combine both.

**Still to test:** Test the trade against a heavy hit: winning outgoing damage is not equivalent to surviving. Carry does not alter the printed trigger.

Current examples: Counter Throw, Rooted Guard, Read the Strike, Trade Blows.

## Checks and limits

The screen retains 8316 possible contexts and 120 comparable modeled pairs. It exits when both directions have witnesses; it is not a full set of matrices. Exact current baselines are shown but deliberately excluded from the draft-only explorer and numerical screen because their additional effects are not fully modeled. New control, replacement, status, and objective families have semantic coverage and explicit tests to perform, not simulated proof of balance.
